# Habit formation: the evidence, including where it contradicts us

Commissioned for Sehaty, with the brief to report contradicting evidence as readily as
supporting evidence. It did, and **three of the six claims I had built into the agent's
prompt were weaker than their reputation or outright wrong.**

Overall verdict from the researcher: *"mediocre, and worse than its citation counts
suggest."* Small samples, WEIRD undergraduate volunteers, self-reported outcomes almost
everywhere, and a consistent pattern — **the best-designed studies most often produce null
or reversed results.**

## What I had wrong

### The lapse rationale
I wrote that shame after a missed day is what kills a habit. **It isn't.**

Kirchner, Shiffman & Wileyto (2012), *J Abnorm Psychol* 121(1):187-197 — 203 smokers,
**1,001 lapse episodes** captured near-real-time by EMA, recurrent-event survival analysis.
The best test of the abstinence violation effect that exists.

- Self-blame, guilt and self-efficacy after a first lapse **did not predict relapse**.
- Increases in internal self-blame were mildly **protective** (HR 0.98, CI 0.97-0.99).
- What *did* predict continued lapsing: **drops in self-efficacy** (HR 1.09, CI 1.02-1.15).

Marlatt & Gordon's relapse-prevention model (1985) is a *theoretical framework*, not an
empirical finding, and has been cited far past its support.

**Design consequence:** after a gap, protect *"you can still do this"* — not *"don't feel
bad."* The rule (never remark on a quiet day) survives; the reason changed.

### Autonomy-supportive wording
Smit, Zeidler, Resnicow & de Vries (2019), *JMIR* 21(10):e14074 — N=525, 2×2, autonomy-
supportive vs controlling language × choice vs no choice.

- Language had **no significant effect on anything**: perceived autonomy support p=.33,
  relevance p=.16, overall evaluation p=.46.
- **Actual choice did work** (b=.12, p=.003).

**Design consequence:** the buttons are the evidence-backed part. Rewriting "you should"
into "you might consider" is the cheap version the evidence does not support.

### Competence beats autonomy
Ng et al. (2012), *Perspectives on Psych Science* 7(4):325-340 — 184 datasets. Competence
satisfaction predicted autonomous motivation **β=.35**, versus autonomy **β=.13** and
relatedness **β=.15**.

**Design consequence:** make someone feel *capable* first. Naming what they can already do
beats any amount of encouragement about effort.

## What held up

- **A missed day does not derail habit formation.** Lally et al. (2010), *Eur J Soc Psychol*
  40(6):998-1009 — missing one opportunity barely moved modelled automaticity and it
  recovered quickly. The anti-streak argument, from the canonical paper.
- **Implementation intentions need reinforcement.** Silva et al. (2018), *PLOS ONE*
  13(11):e0206294 — 13 RCTs. Alone, for physical activity: **d=0.15, non-significant**.
  With reminders/boosters: **0.25, significant**. This is the argument for the reminder
  system, in one line.
- **Intensity hurts adherence; frequency doesn't.** Burnet et al. (2020), *Transl Sports Med*
  3(3) — raising intensity: −3.3% adherence (CI −6.1 to −0.5). Frequency, time, type:
  non-significant.
- **HIIT has no long-term adherence advantage.** Ekkekakis & Biddle (2023), *Psych Sport
  Exerc* 64:102295 — eight trials with ≥12-month follow-up. Unsupervised, people drift to
  lower intensities.
- **Context consistency helps; which cue you pick doesn't.** Zhu et al. (2026), *Appl Psychol
  Health Well-Being* — 14 studies, 6,069 participants, r=.11 behaviour / r=.20 habit
  strength. Small. Keller et al. (2021) RCT, N=192, tested routine-anchored vs time-anchored
  cues directly: **no difference.** Self-selected habits are stronger (Singh 2024).
- **The intention-behaviour gap is 46%**, and it is almost entirely intenders who fail to
  act — 36% of people — not people who never intended. Rhodes & de Bruijn (2013), *Br J
  Health Psychol* 18(2):296-309. The cleanest justification for a tracking app existing.

## Never say these

- **"66 days to form a habit."** Survivor statistic: n=39 of 96 enrolled, and the median for
  *exercise* was 91 days — longer than the study ran. Means across the literature are
  106-154 days, range 4-335. No countdown bars, ever.
- **"Implementation intentions have a large effect."** d=.65 comes from a 2006 book chapter
  by the theory's originators. For physical activity in RCTs it is 0.15 and non-significant.
- **"Guilt after a lapse makes you quit."** Contradicted by the strongest study.
- **"HIIT has better adherence."** Debunked across eight long-follow-up trials.
- The self-compassion **"28g vs 70g sweets"** figure — a secondary-source simplification of
  a three-way interaction in an n=84 undergraduate study whose authors themselves noted the
  control condition may have been food priming.

## Self-compassion, honestly

Real but modest, and smaller than advertised. Phillips & Hine (2021) meta-analysis, 94
studies, N=29,588: self-compassion ↔ health behaviour **r=.26**, mostly cross-sectional.
Thøgersen-Ntoumani et al. (2021): predicted re-engagement *intentions*, **not weight loss**.
Hagerman et al. (2023), N=140: predicted less negative affect and greater perceived control
after a lapse, but **did not predict whether the person lapsed again that day**.
